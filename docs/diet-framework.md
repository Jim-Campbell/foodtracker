# Jim Campbell — Diet Framework

Calibrated for cardiovascular risk (Lp(a) 563, ApoB 89), protein optimization,
and cognitive health. Supersedes standard MIND diet guidelines where evidence
supports.

This document is the **source of truth for food-quality classification** in the
food app. The AI meal parser embeds it verbatim in its system prompt to assign
each food item a tier.

## Hard Yes — Eat freely

- Leafy greens
- Berries
- Nuts
- Legumes
- Whole grains
- Eggs
- Lean chicken
- Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel) — priority for omega-3 index
- Lean fish
- Olive oil
- Vegetables

## Hard No — Avoid

- Alcohol
- Processed meat (bacon, deli meats, sausage, hot dogs)
- Butter
- Fried and fast food
- Ultra-processed foods
- Refined flour / white bread

## Soft Yes — Enjoy in moderation (daily is fine)

- Hard aged cheese (Parmesan, Gouda, aged cheddar, Gruyère) — good K2, protein, leucine
- Fermented dairy: cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, kefir
- Lean unprocessed red meat — grass-fed beef, bison, venison (3-4x/week reasonable)
- Lean unprocessed pork (tenderloin, loin) — comparable to chicken breast in fat content, high B vitamins
- Dark chocolate (70%+)
- Whole grain / whole wheat pasta — lower GI than white, more fiber, counts as whole grain

## Soft No — Occasional, not regular

- Sweets and pastries
- Ice cream
- Soft / processed cheese (American, Velveeta, cheese spreads)
- Fatty cuts of grain-fed beef (ribeye, 80/20 ground beef)
- Fatty pork cuts (shoulder, belly, ribs)
- White flour products

## Notes

- **Protein priority:** Target 150-180g/day given age, low testosterone, and
  anabolic resistance. This framework is compatible with high protein intake —
  eggs, fish, lean meat, Greek yogurt, and legumes are all high-quality protein
  sources. Protein bars (e.g. Pure Protein 20g) are a useful bridge but not a
  dietary foundation.
- **Omega-3 gap:** Omega-3 index was 5.3% (Apr 2024), below the cardiovascular
  protective range of 8-11%. Fatty fish 2-3x/week plus fish oil supplementation
  (2-4g EPA+DHA daily) is the target.
- **Red meat distinction:** The Hard No applies to processed red meat only.
  Unprocessed lean red meat (grass-fed beef, bison, venison) has weak evidence
  for cardiovascular harm at moderate consumption and provides high-quality
  protein, leucine, creatine, zinc, B12, and heme iron. Grass-fed preferred for
  better omega-6:omega-3 ratio.
- **Cheese rationale:** Hard aged cheeses are neutral to modestly beneficial for
  cardiovascular outcomes despite saturated fat content — the food matrix effect
  (calcium binding, K2 content, fermentation) modifies metabolic impact. 1-2 oz
  daily of aged cheese is not a cardiovascular liability. Soft/processed cheese
  lacks these benefits and is high sodium.
- **MIND diet context:** This framework incorporates MIND diet principles but is
  adjusted based on current evidence. The MIND diet's near-elimination of cheese
  and blanket red meat restriction reflects outdated saturated fat dogma. The
  RCT evidence for MIND diet specifically improving cognition is weaker than
  observational studies suggest — exercise has stronger independent evidence for
  cognitive protection.
- **Vitamin K2:** Aged hard cheeses are among the richest dietary sources of K2
  (MK-4/MK-7), which activates matrix Gla protein and may inhibit arterial
  calcification — relevant given CAC score of 24.8 with LAD lesion.
