# Jim Campbell — Diet Framework

*Calibrated for cardiovascular risk (Lp(a) 563, ApoB 89), protein optimization, and cognitive health. Supersedes standard MIND diet guidelines where evidence supports.*

*Version 3 — July 19, 2026. Expanded from a contrast list into a fuller map so that scoring tools do not return "neutral" on unlisted whole foods.*

-----

## Default Rule (read this first)

If a food is **not listed** in this framework, do not score it neutral. Apply this decision cascade instead:

1. **Is it a minimally processed whole plant food?** → Hard Yes.
2. **Is it a lean, unprocessed animal protein?** → Hard Yes (fish, poultry, eggs, shellfish) or Soft Yes (red meat, pork).
3. **Is it high in saturated fat (>~4g per serving) without a fermented/aged food-matrix rationale?** → Soft No.
4. **Is it ultra-processed, deep-fried, refined-flour-based, or sugar-sweetened?** → Hard No.
5. **Otherwise** → Soft Yes, flag for review, and add it to this document.

**Governing principle:** saturated fat and ultra-processing are the two axes that move Jim's cardiovascular risk. Everything else is secondary.

## Hard Yes — Eat freely

**Vegetables & plants**
- Leafy greens
- Cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, kale)
- Vegetables generally
- Mushrooms
- Garlic, onions, herbs, spices
- Fermented vegetables (sauerkraut, kimchi) — *watch sodium*

**Fruit**
- Berries (priority — polyphenols, cognitive evidence)
- **Avocado** — monounsaturated fat, potassium, fiber; strong lipid-profile evidence *(added v2)*
- Olives *(added v2)*
- Whole fruit generally *(added v2)*

**Protein**
- Eggs
- Lean chicken and turkey
- Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel, herring, anchovies) — priority for omega-3 index
- Lean fish (cod, halibut, tilapia, sole)
- **Shellfish** (shrimp, scallops, mussels, oysters, crab) — very lean, high zinc/B12/selenium; dietary cholesterol concern is obsolete *(added v2)*
- Legumes (beans, lentils, chickpeas)
- **Minimally processed soy** (tofu, tempeh, edamame) — complete protein, low saturated fat *(added v2)*

**Fats**
- Olive oil (default cooking and finishing fat)
- **Avocado oil** — high smoke point, monounsaturated *(added v2)*
- Nuts
- **Seeds** (chia, flax, hemp, pumpkin, sunflower) *(added v2)*
- **Natural nut and seed butters** — ingredient list should be nuts + salt only *(added v2)*

**Grains**
- Whole grains (oats, quinoa, farro, brown rice, barley, buckwheat)
- Sprouted whole-grain bread (Ezekiel type) *(added v2)*

**Beverages**
- Water
- **Coffee and tea, unsweetened** — green tea and coffee both carry favorable cardiovascular and cognitive observational data *(added v2)*

- Alcohol
- Processed meat (bacon, deli meats, sausage, hot dogs)
- Butter
- Fried and fast food
- Ultra-processed foods
- Refined flour / white bread
- **Sugar-sweetened beverages** (soda, energy drinks, sweetened coffee drinks) *(added v2)*

## Soft Yes — Enjoy in moderation (daily is fine)

- Hard aged cheese (Parmesan, Gouda, aged cheddar, Gruyère) — good K2, protein, leucine
- Fermented dairy: cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, kefir — full-fat versions are **not** a cardiovascular liability; the reason to default to nonfat/low-fat is protein density within the calorie budget, not saturated fat *(clarified v3 — see note below)*
- Lean unprocessed red meat — grass-fed beef, bison, venison (3-4x/week reasonable)
- Lean unprocessed pork (tenderloin, loin) — comparable to chicken breast in fat content, high B vitamins
- Dark chocolate (70%+)
- Whole grain / whole wheat pasta — lower GI than white, more fiber, counts as whole grain
- Legume-based pasta (chickpea, lentil, edamame) — higher protein and fiber than wheat pasta *(added v2)*
- **Whey or casein protein powder** — legitimate bridge to protein targets; minimal-ingredient isolates preferred; not a foundation *(added v2)*
- **Starchy vegetables** (sweet potato, potato, winter squash, corn) — whole-food quality is fine, but glycemic load matters given HbA1c 5.7%; pair with protein and fat *(added v2)*
- **Dried fruit** — nutritionally intact but sugar-dense and easy to overeat; small portions *(added v2)*
- **Neutral seed oils** (canola, high-oleic sunflower) — acceptable when a neutral-flavor or high-heat oil is needed; olive oil remains the default *(added v2)*
- **Protein bars** (e.g. Pure Protein 20g) — bridge only; most are ultra-processed by any honest definition *(added v2)*

## Soft No — Occasional, not regular

- Sweets and pastries
- Ice cream
- Soft / processed cheese (American, Velveeta, cheese spreads)
- Fatty cuts of grain-fed beef (ribeye, 80/20 ground beef)
- Fatty pork cuts (shoulder, belly, ribs)
- White flour products
- **Coconut oil, coconut cream, full-fat coconut milk** — ~85-90% saturated fat with no fermentation or food-matrix offset; the health-halo marketing is not evidence-based *(added v2)*
- **Palm oil** — high saturated fat, near-ubiquitous in packaged foods *(added v2)*
- **Fruit juice** — fiber removed, sugar concentrated; whole fruit instead *(added v2)*
- **Plant-based meat analogs** (Beyond, Impossible) — ultra-processed, high sodium, often coconut/palm fat; the plant framing does not exempt them *(added v2)*
- **Non-fermented full-fat dairy** (whole milk, cream, half-and-half) — the fermentation and food-matrix argument that rescues aged cheese and yogurt does not extend to these *(added v2, narrowed v3)*
- **High-sodium packaged foods** — canned soups, frozen entrées, most restaurant sauces *(added v2)*

## Notes

**Category is not calorie budget.** *(new in v2 — important for the scoring app.)* "Hard Yes" describes nutritional quality, not permission for unlimited quantity. Avocado, nuts, seeds, nut butters, and olive oil are all Hard Yes and all calorie-dense — a whole avocado is ~320 calories, two tablespoons of almond butter ~190. At a 1,800-1,900 cal/day target with 160-180g protein, these need portioning even though they score well. **Recommendation for the app: score food quality and calorie/protein fit on two separate axes rather than collapsing them into one number.** A food can be a Hard Yes and still be the wrong choice on a given day.

**Avocado specifically:** Established — avocado consumption modestly lowers LDL and ApoB in controlled feeding trials, largely via monounsaturated fat displacing saturated fat. Inference — this is directionally useful for Jim's ApoB 89 but the effect size is small relative to statin/ezetimibe. Opinion — its real value is as a satiety and texture tool that makes lean, high-protein eating sustainable.

**Protein priority:** Target 150-180g/day given age, low testosterone, and anabolic resistance. This framework is compatible with high protein intake — eggs, fish, shellfish, lean meat, Greek yogurt, soy, and legumes are all high-quality protein sources. Protein bars are a useful bridge but not a dietary foundation.

**Omega-3 gap:** Omega-3 index was 5.3% (Apr 2024), below the cardiovascular protective range of 8-11%. Fatty fish 2-3x/week plus fish oil supplementation (2-4g EPA+DHA daily) is the target. **Recheck overdue — reference figure is now over two years old; slated for August 2026 labs.**

**Red meat distinction:** The Hard No applies to *processed* red meat only. Unprocessed lean red meat (grass-fed beef, bison, venison) has weak evidence for cardiovascular harm at moderate consumption and provides high-quality protein, leucine, creatine, zinc, B12, and heme iron. Grass-fed preferred for better omega-6:omega-3 ratio.

**Full-fat fermented dairy:** *(new in v3.)* Established — meta-analyses of fermented dairy show neutral-to-favorable cardiovascular associations, and stratification by fat content generally does not show full-fat performing worse; yogurt is the most-studied case. The fermentation and food-matrix rationale that applies to aged cheese applies to yogurt and kefir as well. Inference — full-fat yogurt is therefore not a cardiovascular problem for Jim, and the earlier v2 Soft No entry was an overreach. The reason to default to nonfat Greek yogurt is arithmetic: ¾ cup nonfat is ~100 cal / ~18g protein, full-fat 5% is ~155 cal / ~15g protein. More protein for fewer calories matters at 170g inside 1,850 cal — the same logic that favors chicken breast over thigh. Opinion — use full-fat when it is doing culinary work (sauce base, sour cream replacement, or where texture determines whether the meal actually gets eaten); default to nonfat for the daily protein-vehicle role.

**Cheese rationale:** Hard aged cheeses are neutral to modestly beneficial for cardiovascular outcomes despite saturated fat content — the food matrix effect (calcium binding, K2 content, fermentation) modifies metabolic impact. 1-2 oz daily of aged cheese is not a cardiovascular liability. Soft/processed cheese lacks these benefits and is high sodium. **This rationale is specific to aged, fermented cheese and does not generalize to other full-fat dairy.**

**Seed oil position:** *(new in v2.)* Established — no strong human outcome evidence that canola or sunflower oil causes cardiovascular harm; replacing saturated fat with them improves lipid markers. Opinion — the anti-seed-oil movement is currently outrunning its evidence. Practical stance: olive oil is the default because the evidence base for it is deeper, not because seed oils are dangerous.

**Shellfish and dietary cholesterol:** *(new in v2.)* Established — dietary cholesterol has a weak effect on serum cholesterol in most people; the 2015 US dietary guidelines dropped the limit. Shrimp and scallops are among the leanest high-protein foods available and are well suited to the calorie-constrained, protein-dense pattern Jim is running.

**MIND diet context:** This framework incorporates MIND diet principles but is adjusted based on current evidence. The MIND diet's near-elimination of cheese and blanket red meat restriction reflects outdated saturated fat dogma. The RCT evidence for MIND diet specifically improving cognition is weaker than observational studies suggest — exercise has stronger independent evidence for cognitive protection.

**Vitamin K2:** Aged hard cheeses are among the richest dietary sources of K2 (MK-4/MK-7), which activates matrix Gla protein and may inhibit arterial calcification — relevant given CAC score of 24.8 with LAD lesion.
